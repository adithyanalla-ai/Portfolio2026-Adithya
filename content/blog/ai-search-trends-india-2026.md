If you feel like everyone around you has suddenly started talking about AI, the data agrees with you. According to Google Trends, search interest in the term **"AI" in India has grown by roughly 50% compared with the preceding year**, and it has stayed consistently high through 2026 with one dramatic spike in the autumn.

But the headline number hides the more interesting story. People in India are no longer typing just "AI". They are searching for specific tools, specific brands and specific creative workflows: Gemini, Google Flow, Arena AI, Meigen AI, OpenFuture and even daily "AI news today" updates.

## How to read this data (a quick note on Google Trends)

Before diving in, a short caveat that will make you smarter than most people quoting Trends screenshots:

> [!NOTE] Trends numbers are relative, not absolute
> A score of 100 is the peak popularity for the selected region and time, and every other point is scaled against it. It does not tell you how many searches happened.

- **"Top queries"** are the most searched related terms. The percentage beside them shows growth versus the previous period.
- **"Rising queries"** are terms with the biggest growth. A label of **"Breakout"** means the term grew so fast (by more than 5,000%) that Google does not print a percentage. Often that is because it started from a very small base.
- **Small bases make big percentages.** A term growing 1,650% can still have far lower total volume than a term growing 30%.

Keep that in mind as you read the numbers below. They show direction and momentum, not market size.

## AI interest over time in India: steady growth and one big spike

The interest-over-time chart for "AI" in India tells a clear story:

- **Year-on-year growth of about +50%** versus the preceding year.
- Interest began the period at around 60 on the 0-100 scale, then climbed to the low 70s within the first couple of months.
- It stepped up again in the spring, reaching the mid-80s, and then held in a remarkably stable band of roughly **78 to 84** through the summer.
- A sharp spike to the **peak of 100** appeared in early autumn, likely tied to a major product launch or news event, before settling back to the low 70s and recovering to about **82** in the latest reading.

**What this means:** AI is no longer a spike-driven news topic in India. It has become a permanent, high baseline of everyday curiosity. The summer plateau is the important signal. Even with no major event, Indians searched for AI at close to peak levels week after week.

For content creators, that plateau is good news. It means evergreen AI content (guides, comparisons, tutorials) has a stable audience, while news-driven content can ride the occasional spikes.

### 1. Gemini dominates Indian AI search

The first three positions are all variations of **Gemini**, each with the longest search-interest bar and 30% growth. Add "google ai" at number four (+20%), and Google's AI ecosystem owns the top of the list.

Why might that be? A few plausible reasons:

- **Android reach.** India is overwhelmingly an Android market, so Gemini is a short tap away for hundreds of millions of people.
- **Google Search integration.** AI features inside the tool people already use daily lower the barrier to trying them.
- **Student and creator offers and bundles** that put Google's AI plans in front of younger users.

Treat those as reasonable explanations rather than proven causes. The Trends data shows *what* people search, not *why*.

### 2. Claude is the fastest-growing major assistant

The most striking line in the entire table is **"claude" at +400% and "claude ai" at +350%**. These are the largest growth numbers among the established assistants in the top ten, even though their search-interest bars are shorter than Gemini's.

That pattern (smaller base, enormous growth) is typical of a tool that is rapidly gaining mindshare. Developers, writers, analysts and students in India appear to be discovering Claude in large numbers, which fits the broader global conversation around AI coding assistants and long-document work.

### 3. ChatGPT is still searched, but growth has cooled

"chatgpt ai" (-4%) and "ai chatgpt" (-20%) remain in the top ten, which tells us ChatGPT is still a household name. But the negative change suggests **search growth has plateaued or slightly declined** for these specific phrasings, while attention spreads across competing assistants.

That does not mean ChatGPT usage is falling. People increasingly search "ChatGPT" alone, use the app directly, or type other phrasings. It does mean the market is no longer a one-horse race in the minds of Indian searchers.

### 4. Visual AI: video up, photo down

- **"ai video" (+20%)** is growing, which lines up with the rise of generative video tools.
- **"ai photo" (-30%)** is falling, which suggests that the novelty of AI image generation has worn off, and that people now search for specific tools or workflows rather than the generic phrase.

In other words, the creative frontier of Indian AI search has moved from still images to moving images.

Let's unpack what each of these is.

### Google Flow (and "Flow AI"): the clear theme of the rising list

Four of the ten rising queries are about Flow: "google flow ai", "google flow", "flow ai" and simply "flow". Together they show a single trend, which is **Indians discovering Google's AI filmmaking tool**.

**What is Google Flow?** It is Google's AI-powered filmmaking tool. It builds on Google's generative models to let you create cinematic clips and scenes from text or image prompts, with controls for camera movement, scene continuity and asset management. It also comes with Flow TV, a gallery of clips made with Flow along with the prompts behind them, which doubles as a learning resource.

Several India-specific details help explain the surge:

- Google's own help documentation notes that Flow requires a paid Google AI plan, an age-verified account, and availability in your region.
- Clips made by users in India carry a **visible watermark**, in addition to the invisible SynthID watermark Google embeds in AI-generated content.
- Flow has an Indian film-industry connection: Women in Film India, led by filmmaker Guneet Monga Kapoor, has partnered with Google on the **WIF India x Google Flow Filmathon**, where filmmakers of any level can create AI-assisted stories. The top five films are slated to be showcased at the International Film Festival of India (IFFI) in Goa in 2026.

That Filmathon alone could explain a meaningful part of the interest from creators, students and filmmakers.

### "Wisk AI": most likely Google's Whisk

The spelling "wisk ai" is almost certainly people searching for **Whisk**, a Google Labs experiment launched in December 2024. Whisk takes a different approach to image generation: instead of writing long text prompts, you upload images to define the **subject, scene and style**, and the tool blends them into something new. It uses Gemini to caption your images and Google's Imagen model to generate the result.

The misspelling itself is a content opportunity. If you publish a Whisk tutorial, include the common variant "wisk" naturally in a heading or FAQ so you capture people who search the way they spell.

### Arena AI: the AI model scoreboard

"Arena AI" is the new name of what many people know as **LMArena (originally Chatbot Arena)**. It was created by researchers from UC Berkeley and works like a tournament. You send one prompt, two anonymous AI models answer, and you vote for the better response. Those votes feed a public leaderboard.

The platform has expanded well beyond text. Its leaderboards now cover areas like web development, vision, image generation, image editing, search and text-to-video, and it shows practical details such as pricing and context window alongside rankings.

Why is it rising in India? Because with so many assistants competing, people want an independent answer to the question "which AI is actually best for my task?" Arena gives a crowd-sourced, real-use answer rather than a company's own benchmark claims.

### Meigen AI: the prompt-gallery breakout

**Meigen AI** appears as a "Breakout" query. Descriptions of the product vary a little across sources, but the common thread is clear: it is a **library of ready-made prompts for AI image and video generation**, with previews of real outputs and one-click copy, remix and generate options. It works with popular image and video models, and it runs on a credit-based system with a free way to browse.

Think of it as a "Pinterest for prompts". Its rise tells us that Indian creators, marketers and small businesses want **shortcuts to good results** instead of learning prompt engineering from scratch.

### OpenFuture AI: the tools directory

**OpenFuture** (openfuture.ai) is an **AI tools directory**. It lists and categorises AI tools across more than 40 categories, from chat and image generation to writing and productivity, so people can discover and compare them. It belongs to the same family as other AI directories.

Its breakout status shows that people are overwhelmed by the sheer number of AI products and are looking for a map.

### "AI editor rsp editing": a niche term to watch

This query is a Breakout, but its meaning is not clear from public sources. It may be tied to a specific editing app, a regional creator trend, or a niche community. If you work in video or photo editing content, it is worth searching it yourself and checking the related results before writing about it. I am deliberately not guessing here, because a wrong explanation hurts credibility.

### "AI news today": the daily habit

"ai news today" at **+1,450%** shows that Indians are forming a **daily habit of following AI developments**. This is a strong signal for publishers. A regularly updated AI news roundup, newsletter or blog section can build a loyal returning audience.

## Which Indian states search for AI the most?

The regional breakdown for the past year ranks the top states and territories by search interest:

1. **Telangana**
2. **Andhra Pradesh**
3. **Puducherry**
4. **Tamil Nadu**
5. **Delhi**

The regional map shows deeper blue shading across the south and parts of the west, which means search interest is highest across southern India.

This pattern is consistent with where India's major technology hubs, IT workforces, engineering colleges and startup ecosystems are concentrated. Hyderabad, Bengaluru and Chennai anchor a huge share of the country's software talent, so it is natural that curiosity about AI runs highest there. Delhi's presence reflects the capital region's large professional and student population.

Note that Google Trends ranks regions by *proportion of searches*, not total volume. A smaller state can outrank a larger one if a bigger share of its searches are about AI.

**Content tip:** If you serve a local audience, add regional angles. Titles like "AI tools for Hyderabad startups" or "AI courses for students in Telangana and Andhra Pradesh" align with where the interest actually is.

## 7 key takeaways from the India AI search data

1. **AI interest is mainstream and stable.** Roughly +50% year on year, with a steady plateau rather than a one-off spike.
2. **Gemini and Google AI lead the top queries**, helped by Google's distribution.
3. **Claude is surging.** +400% growth makes it the standout in the assistant category.
4. **Search has moved from generic to specific.** People are naming tools (Flow, Whisk, Arena, Meigen) rather than typing "AI photo".
5. **Video is the new frontier.** "ai video" is up while "ai photo" is down, and Flow dominates the rising list.
6. **Discovery and comparison tools are rising.** Arena (rankings), OpenFuture (directory) and Meigen (prompt library) all reflect a need to navigate a crowded market.
7. **South India leads.** Telangana, Andhra Pradesh and Tamil Nadu are among the top regions.

## What this means for different audiences

### For bloggers and content creators

- Build **evergreen pillar pages** ("Best AI tools in India", "Gemini vs Claude vs ChatGPT") because baseline interest is stable.
- Publish **fast, accurate explainers** on rising terms (Flow, Whisk, Arena, Meigen) while competition is still low.
- Include **spelling variants** such as "wisk" in headings and FAQs.
- Add a **daily or weekly AI news** section to capture the "ai news today" habit.

### For marketers and agencies

- Invest in **AI video** workflows. Search behaviour shows the audience is moving that way.
- Use prompt libraries and model leaderboards to speed up creative production, but always review outputs for brand fit and legal safety.
- Remember that AI-generated media from tools like Flow carries watermarks for users in India, so plan creative accordingly.

### For developers and AI engineers

- Track Claude's momentum alongside Gemini and ChatGPT, and avoid locking your product to a single provider.
- Use public leaderboards like Arena as one input for model selection, then validate on your own data and tasks.

### For students and job seekers

- The south Indian concentration of interest suggests strong local communities, meetups and hiring around AI. Build projects, publish them, and learn tools hands-on rather than only watching tutorials.

### For founders and businesses

- Rising directory and gallery searches show a **discovery gap**. If you build an AI product, make sure you are listed in directories and publish clear comparison and how-to content.

## How to use Google Trends yourself (step by step)

You can repeat this research for your own niche in a few minutes:

1. Open Google Trends and go to **Explore**.
2. Enter your keyword (for example, "AI").
3. Set the location to **India** and the time range to **Past 12 months** (or 5 years for long-term patterns).
4. Scroll to **Interest by region** to see which states care most.
5. Check **Related queries**, then switch between **Top** and **Rising** to see steady demand versus emerging trends.
6. Compare multiple terms, such as "Gemini", "Claude" and "ChatGPT", to see relative strength.
7. Download the data for your own analysis and revisit it monthly.

## Limitations and honest caveats

- Trends data reflects **Google Search only**. Many people now ask AI assistants directly instead of searching, so real-world usage may be larger than search data suggests.
- Percentages for small-volume terms can look dramatic. Always confirm with other sources before making business decisions.
- Different spellings, languages and brand names are split across queries, so totals for a single tool are understated.
- This article is based on a snapshot of Google Trends for India over the past year as of October 2026. Numbers will change.

Google Flow is an AI filmmaking tool from Google that lets users generate cinematic clips and scenes from text or image prompts, with camera control and scene-building features. It requires a paid Google AI plan and is available in selected regions.

Arena AI (formerly LMArena) is a community-powered platform created by UC Berkeley researchers. Users compare anonymous AI model responses side by side and vote, producing a public leaderboard of model performance.
